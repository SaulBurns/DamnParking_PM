Project title

Damn Parking — camera-based parking occupancy intelligence for a campus pilot lot.

Purpose

Deliver a managed semester project that designs (and where feasible, prototypes the information architecture for) a system which detects open vs. occupied parking bays from lot camera imagery and publishes availability to drivers and parking services.

Objectives

  • Define a clear MVP: bay-level occupancy for one pilot lot, daylight-first, with explicit accuracy and latency targets to be refined in Sprint 2 estimation.
  • Maintain a living project portal that records research, strategy, charter, and later business case / ROI artifacts.
  • Surface risks early: privacy/policy, lighting, occlusion, and integration with campus IT.

Scope boundary

In scope

  • Problem framing, market research, strategy, and charter (Sprint 1).
  • Business case, estimation, and ROI analysis (Sprint 2+ as assigned).
  • Conceptual architecture for CV occupancy → API/event stream → driver map + ops dashboard.
  • UX narrative and portal visualization that communicates the product vision (simulated live lot).
  • Risk register and stakeholder engagement notes as the portal grows.

Out of scope

  • Production deployment on live campus cameras without formal approval.
  • Payment, permitting, ticketing, or LPR enforcement systems.
  • Autonomous vehicles, robotic valet, or stall-reservation marketplaces.
  • Installing new camera hardware campus-wide (may note single-lot gap fill only).
  • Publishing Sprint Retrospectives or peer evaluations on the public site.

Assumptions

  1. At least one pilot lot has (or can be granted) camera coverage with angles sufficient for bay inference, or the team will document coverage gaps and use representative video/datasets for coursework prototypes.
  2. Parking services is the primary institutional sponsor persona; students/staff drivers are primary end users of the availability view.
  3. Privacy and data-retention rules can be satisfied with occupancy metadata (bay states) rather than long-term storage of identifiable face/plate imagery in the MVP design.
  4. The living portal remains the system of record for public PM artifacts; Blackboard holds private retros, peer evals, and PDF submission copies.
  5. Team members can explain any portal page in final Q&A, regardless of original authorship.

Constraints

  • Course calendar and sprint deadlines; portal content must be live by demo time.
  • Free hosting + public GitHub repository required.
  • Every public document must exist as on-page text and downloadable PDF.
  • No live publication of retrospectives or peer evaluations.

Stakeholder register

Stakeholder Interest Influence Engagement
Campus parking / facilities Occupancy truth, congestion, pilot feasibility High Primary sponsor persona; validate scope & policy
Student & staff drivers Faster time-to-park, less circling Medium Interviews, usability of availability UX
Campus IT / security Camera access, privacy, network High Gate on feeds; retention rules
Course instructor PM quality, portal completeness, demos High Sprint reviews; grading artifacts
Project team Clear ownership, defensible decisions High Daily execution; portal upkeep
Peer review teams Gallery-walk feedback on Sprint 1 Low–Med Structured critique; we respond & revise

High-level risks (charter level)

  • Policy / privacy block on camera access → mitigate with synthetic/demo video and explicit metadata-only design.
  • CV accuracy under glare, night, occlusion → daylight pilot, confidence thresholds, human override path.
  • Scope creep into payments or enforcement → charter out-of-scope list enforced in backlog grooming.
  • Portal drift (docs without PDFs, or PDFs without web text) → checklist owner each sprint.

Success criteria (semester)

  • Living portal remains complete and coherent sprint-over-sprint.
  • Charter stays accurate; change logs (Sprint 2+) explain drift.
  • Business case / estimation / ROI (when required) tie back to this scope boundary.
  • Team can defend every public page in presentation Q&A.
